MOA & AOA drafting for your company.

The Memorandum and Articles of Association (MOA/AOA) are the founding documents SECP requires for a private limited company or SMC, defining your company’s objectives, share structure, and internal rules.

Two documents, two jobs

What each one actually covers

Memorandum of Association (MOA)

States your company's name, registered office, objectives (what business activities you're allowed to conduct), and the amount and division of share capital. SECP checks that your later business activity stays within what the MOA allows.

Articles of Association (AOA)

The internal rulebook — how directors are appointed, how shares can be transferred, how meetings are conducted, and how disputes between shareholders are resolved.

Common questions

Answered

What's the difference between MOA and AOA?

The Memorandum of Association (MOA) defines your company's objectives and scope of business. The Articles of Association (AOA) set the internal rules for how the company is run — director powers, share transfers, and meeting procedures.

Do sole proprietorships need an MOA/AOA?

No. MOA/AOA are required only for companies registering with SECP, such as a private limited company or SMC — sole proprietorships don't file either document.

Can I use a generic MOA/AOA template?

You can, but a generic template may not reflect your actual business activity or intended share structure, which can cause problems later — for example, if your MOA doesn't cover an activity you later expand into.
