How sole proprietors file taxes in Pakistan.

Sole proprietors file taxes as individuals under their personal NTN, reporting business income directly on their personal income tax return — there’s no separate company return, since there’s no separate legal entity.

Simpler, but not optional

One return covers everything

Since a sole proprietorship has no separate legal identity, all business income, expenses, and tax owed flow into your personal income tax return — there's no second filing to keep track of, but the numbers still need to be right.

If your business grows to the point where liability protection matters, converting to an SMC creates a genuinely separate entity, with its own NTN and its own return going forward.

Common questions

Answered

Do sole proprietors file a separate business tax return?

No — a sole proprietor's business income is reported directly on their personal income tax return with FBR, since there's no separate legal entity to file for.

Can a sole proprietor claim business expenses against their income?

Yes, legitimate business expenses can generally be deducted from business income before calculating the tax owed, similar to how a company deducts its expenses.

Does a sole proprietor need to register for sales tax too?

Only if their business supplies taxable goods or services above FBR's threshold — the same sales tax rules that apply to companies apply to sole proprietors.
