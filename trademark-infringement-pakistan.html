What to do if someone copies your registered brand.

Trademark infringement occurs when someone uses a mark identical or confusingly similar to your registered trademark without permission. A registered owner can pursue civil remedies including injunctions and damages through Pakistan’s IP tribunals.

Why registration matters here

Enforcement is far easier once you're registered

An unregistered mark can still be defended through a common-law "passing off" claim, but you'll need to prove your brand had already built up a reputation that the infringer is trading on — a higher and more expensive bar than simply pointing to a registration certificate.

If you believe your registered trademark is being infringed, the usual first step is a cease-and-desist notice, followed by formal proceedings before Pakistan's IP tribunals if that doesn't resolve it.

Common questions

Answered

What counts as trademark infringement in Pakistan?

Using a mark that's identical or confusingly similar to a registered trademark, on the same or related goods/services, without the owner's permission, in a way likely to confuse consumers.

Can I take action against infringement if my trademark isn't registered?

It's significantly harder — unregistered marks rely on common-law passing-off claims, which require proving established reputation, whereas registration gives you a clear statutory right to enforce.

What remedies are available against an infringer?

Registered trademark owners can typically pursue civil remedies including injunctions to stop the infringing use, damages, and account of profits, through Pakistan's IP tribunals.
