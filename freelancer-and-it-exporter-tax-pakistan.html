Freelancer tax filing in Pakistan: foreign income, IT exports and PSEB

Freelancers in Pakistan should register an NTN, keep records of foreign payments and file a return each year. IT exporters and software houses can fall under a special export tax regime, and PSEB registration is commonly used to support that treatment. Confirm eligibility for your business.

What do freelancers in Pakistan need to do?

  1. Register an NTN: NTN registration in Pakistan.
  2. Keep proof of foreign remittances (bank advices, platform statements).
  3. File a return each year: income tax filing in Pakistan.
  4. Confirm you appear on the ATL: Active Taxpayer List (ATL) check by CNIC.

Is foreign income taxable for freelancers?

Income earned from clients abroad and received in Pakistan is generally reportable. Special reduced-rate regimes apply to certain IT and IT-enabled exports; the rate is set in each Finance Act, so verify the current rate with FBR or a consultant.

IT exporter tax in Pakistan and PSEB registration

Software houses and IT service exporters often register with the Pakistan Software Export Board (PSEB) to document export activity. Check PSEB's current criteria and how they connect to your tax treatment before relying on either.

Company or freelancer: which structure is better?

Many freelancers become a company or SMC as revenue grows, for credibility and liability protection. See SMC (single member company) registration in Pakistan and sole proprietorship registration in Pakistan.

Common freelancer mistakes

  • Not reporting remittances that match bank statements.
  • Mixing personal and business accounts.
  • Not filing because income seems small.

Questions people ask about freelancer tax

Do I have to pay tax if I earn in dollars?

Foreign-currency income received in Pakistan is generally taxable, subject to the applicable regime.

Do freelancers need sales tax registration?

Services are usually handled by provincial rules. See provincial sales tax.

Can I use a personal account for client payments?

You can, but separate business records make filing easier.
