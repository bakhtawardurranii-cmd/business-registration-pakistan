You have your SECP certificate. Now what?

SECP incorporation makes your company a legal entity, but it doesn’t register you with FBR. Before you can invoice, bank, or pay taxes, you need to separately register your company with FBR through the IRIS portal to get its NTN.

The most common confusion

Two registrations, not one

A lot of new founders assume that once SECP issues a certificate of incorporation, the company is fully "registered" and ready to go. In practice, that certificate only covers the legal-entity side. FBR registration — a separate step entirely — is what actually lets the company function: invoice clients, open a bank account, and pay taxes.

The handoff is straightforward once you know it's required: take your certificate of incorporation, MOA/AOA, and director CNICs straight into an FBR IRIS registration for your company's NTN, ideally within days of incorporation rather than weeks.

Common questions

Answered

Why isn't SECP incorporation enough on its own?

SECP incorporation only creates your company as a legal entity — it doesn't register you with FBR, so without a separate FBR registration your company legally exists but can't operate, invoice, or pay taxes.

How soon after getting my SECP certificate should I register with FBR?

Immediately — most banks won't open a corporate account, and you can't legally invoice clients, until your company has an NTN from FBR.

Do I need to submit my SECP certificate to FBR?

Yes, your certificate of incorporation is one of the core documents FBR requires to register your company and issue its NTN.
